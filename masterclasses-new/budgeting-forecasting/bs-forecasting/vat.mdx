Forecast VAT by modelling two movements: sales and cost VAT captured during the month, and settlements paid to the authorities.

Balance sheet forecasting is always a function of last period's value, additions, and detractions — see [BS fundamentals](/masterclasses-new/budgeting-forecasting/bs-forecasting/bs-fundamentals). For VAT, this means VAT payable last period + sales VAT in period − cost VAT in period − VAT paid to authorities.

## Sales and cost VAT captured in the month

Sales VAT and cost VAT typically sit on separate GL accounts and are forecast separately.

To forecast captured **sales VAT**, pick up every P&L and balance sheet income line that includes VAT and multiply by the relevant rate (25% in Denmark). That gives you the sales VAT addition for the month.

Cost VAT works the same way: pick up every P&L and balance sheet expense line that includes VAT, multiply by the rate, and you have the cost VAT addition for the month.

### Approximating instead of mapping every account

Each GL account carries its own VAT code, but most finance teams don't need that granularity in their forecast. The usual approximation is to either:

- Pick the specific [groups or rows](/features-new/using-francis/components) known to include VAT, or
- Take a percentage of broader groups or rows where VAT exposure is mixed

Both are accurate enough for cash flow forecasting — mapping every VAT code rarely changes the answer.

<Tip>
Put the **Sales incl. VAT** and **Cost incl. VAT** subtotals in a separate sheet as an intermediate calculation. It keeps the VAT logic out of the P&L and makes the dependency explicit, and the VAT lines on the balance sheet reference those subtotals directly.
</Tip>

Once the captured amounts are in place, add them to the VAT forecast for the month.
