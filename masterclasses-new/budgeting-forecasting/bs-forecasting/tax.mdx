Corporate tax is typically settled once or twice a year. Keep the forecast simple: roll the balance forward, hardcode known payments in the month they fall due, and let the cash flow statement pick up the impact automatically.

Balance sheet forecasting is always a function of last period's value, additions, and detractions. See [BS fundamentals](/masterclasses-new/budgeting-forecasting/bs-forecasting/bs-fundamentals). For tax, this means tax payable last period + tax expense recognized in period − tax payments made to authorities.

## Before you start

Map your corporate tax payable account in **Account mapping** before forecasting. The roll-forward carries last period's actuals forward. If the account isn't mapped, the opening balance is zero and the forecast will be wrong.

## Roll-forward with payment event

Carry last period's balance forward. In the month the payment falls due, the balance clears to zero and the cash flow statement picks up the outflow automatically.

```typescript
= if(if_month(11) = 1, 0, "Corporate tax payable"[-1])
```

This formula reads: if it's November, return zero. Otherwise, carry last period's balance forward. The drop in the balance is what Francis uses to derive the cash outflow. No manual cash flow entry needed.

<Note>
Adjust the month number to match your payment schedule. If you have two payment events in the year, use `if_month(3, 11)` and set the balance to zero in both months, or hardcode the residual balance after the first payment.
</Note>

## FAQ

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="Do I need to account for deferred tax?">
    Keep deferred tax as a separate balance sheet line. Hold the balance flat in the forecast unless a specific event is expected to trigger a material movement. Don't forecast it the same way as corporate tax payable.
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>
