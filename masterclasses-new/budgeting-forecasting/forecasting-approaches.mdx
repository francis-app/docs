Before you build, decide how you'll forecast. There are three approaches in Francis — most models use a combination of all three depending on the line item.

## Driver-based

Build calculations linked to underlying assumptions. Revenue grows at a rate, costs scale as a percentage of revenue, headcount drives salary costs. When an assumption changes, everything connected to it updates automatically.

Use this for anything you expect to revisit and adjust — revenue, headcount, working capital. Driver-based models take longer to build but produce forecasts that are easier to stress-test and explain to stakeholders.

## Trend-based

Project forward from historical actuals. Francis looks at past performance — moving averages, year-on-year patterns, last twelve months — and uses that as the baseline. Use this when the near-term future is expected to mirror recent performance and you have enough actuals history to project from.

Trend-based forecasting is the fastest to set up and works well for stable, recurring line items. It becomes less reliable when the business has changed significantly or when you're forecasting a line item with limited history.

## Hardcoded

Enter numbers directly. No formula, no driver — just a value. Fast to set up but static, meaning changes elsewhere in the model don't flow through.

Use hardcoded entries for fixed-cost items with no meaningful driver, or as a starting point when importing an existing budget into Francis.
