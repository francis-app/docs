The P&L is the center of gravity in most Francis models. Actuals flow in from your accounting system; the forecast builds forward from the structure you set.

## The Francis P&L template

The template is a starting point. Add rows, rename groups, remove metrics you don't use. The structure is yours to adapt. If your business reports contribution margin instead of gross margin, change it. If you don't track EBITDA, remove it.

Gross margin, EBITDA margin, and net profit margin are pre-built calculations. In the annual total column these show as weighted averages rather than sums, which is the correct treatment when margins vary month to month.

Map your GL accounts to the relevant rows before you start forecasting. Actuals won't populate until the mapping is in place.

## How P&L forecasting works

Each row in the P&L can use a different forecasting approach. Revenue rows might use a growth rate or a driver-based model. OPEX rows might be hardcoded or linked to headcount. The structure is set once; the method is chosen per row. Actuals from your accounting system populate automatically once GL accounts are mapped, and sit alongside the forecast in the same view. Each line item page covers which forecasting approaches work best for that row.

## Connected across sheets

The P&L is the source that the Balance Sheet and cash flow draw from. Net profit flows into retained earnings on the Balance Sheet. Depreciation flows into the cash flow statement as a non-cash add-back.
