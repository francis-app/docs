Salary forecasting has two characteristics that make it worth keeping off the main P&L: it's complex enough (components, hire dates, annual increases) to make the P&L unreadable if forecast directly, and sensitive enough that many CFOs want it out of shared views.

## Set up a headcount sheet

Create a dedicated sheet. Group by department, with rows for each employee or role beneath. Use a single aggregated salary row per department if you don't need that level of detail.

<Tip>If you use dimensions, you can break down actuals by department or employee without splitting GL accounts into separate rows (useful if your chart of accounts doesn't map cleanly to your headcount structure).</Tip>

## Salary increases

For planned increases, reference the prior month and apply a growth rate in the relevant month using `if_month()`:

```typescript
= "Base salary"[-1] * (1 + if_month(1) * 5%)
```

This applies a 5% increase in January. Adjust the month and percentage to match your review cycle.

## Connecting to the P&L

P&L OPEX rows reference the headcount sheet rather than containing their own salary forecasts. When you reforecast on the headcount sheet (a new hire, a revised increase), the P&L updates automatically. All headcount forecasting happens in one place.

<Note>As you reforecast and move the forecast start date forward, periods that were forecast become actuals. Enter actual salary values for those periods in the headcount sheet. Formulas referencing prior months, such as salary increases, will break if the preceding actuals period is empty.</Note>
