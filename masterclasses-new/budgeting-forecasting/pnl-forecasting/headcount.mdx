Salary forecasting is complex enough (components, hire dates, annual increases) to make the P&L unreadable if forecast directly, and sensitive enough that many CFOs want it out of shared views. A dedicated headcount sheet keeps the complexity contained and the P&L clean.

## Set up the sheet structure

Create a dedicated sheet. A common structure has two sections: **Assumptions** at the top and salary totals below.

In the **Assumptions** section, add rows for variables that apply across all employees: salary increase percentage, pension percentage, or any other shared inputs. These become the single inputs that drive calculations across the sheet.

For the salary section, group by department. A common approach adds a group per employee or role underneath, with rows for base salary, pension, and other employer costs. The exact structure depends on what your business tracks. You might group by role rather than individual, or use a single aggregated row per department if you don't need that level of detail.

<Tip>If you use dimensions, you can break down actuals by department or employee without splitting GL accounts into separate rows, which is useful if your chart of accounts doesn't map cleanly to your headcount structure.</Tip>

## Enter starting salaries

For each current employee, enter their monthly salary directly on the **Salary** row. This is the baseline the forecast builds from.

## Forecast ongoing salaries

On each salary row, reference the prior month and apply the salary increase assumption:

```typescript
= "Salary"[-1] + ("Salary"[-1] * "Salary increase"[0])
```

In months where the salary increase assumption is zero, the salary carries forward unchanged. When the assumption fires, the increase is applied automatically.

## Model salary increases

The `if_month()` logic lives on the **Salary increase** row in the Assumptions section, not on the salary row itself:

```typescript
= if_month(6) * 3%
```

This sets the increase to 3% in month 6 and zero in all other months. Adjust the month number and percentage to match your review cycle. Because every salary row references the same assumption, updating it in one place updates every employee automatically.

## Add pension and employer costs

Reference the salary row and apply the pension percentage from the Assumptions section:

```typescript
= "Salary"[0] * "Pension %"[0]
```

Add a row for other salary-related costs using the same pattern with the relevant percentage.

## Model new hires

For a new hire joining mid-year, leave the **Salary** row empty for months before their start date. Enter their salary directly in the month they join, then apply the ongoing salary formula from the following month forward.

## Connect to the P&L

On the relevant row in the P&L, add a formula that references the total salary cost from the headcount sheet:

```typescript
= "Total salary cost"[0]
```

When you reforecast on the headcount sheet (a new hire, a revised increase), the P&L updates automatically. All headcount forecasting happens in one place.

<Note>As you move the Forecast Start date forward, periods that were forecast become actuals. Enter actual salary values for those periods on the headcount sheet. Formulas referencing prior months will break if the preceding actuals period is empty.</Note>
