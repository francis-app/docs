Applying an entity breakdown sets up your consolidation. Each entity gets its own sub-sheet, and actuals and forecasts roll up to the consolidated template automatically. Sub-consolidations let you add views below the group level for legal holding structures, regional groupings, or any other slice of the group that needs its own view. See [Breakdowns](/features-new/using-francis/breakdowns) to apply your entity breakdown first.

## Common use cases

### Sub-consolidations for legal structures

If your group has a holding entity owning operating entities, create a roll-up for each layer. Open the action menu (...) on the consolidation template, choose **Add rollup**, and drag the relevant entity sub-sheets into it. Repeat for each layer. Each layer gets its own clean consolidated view without duplicating the model structure.

### Regional splits

Group entities by geography using sub-consolidations. Add an EMEA roll-up and an Asia roll-up, then drag the relevant entity sub-sheets into each. The top-level consolidated sheet rolls up all regions. You get a regional P&L alongside the group view in the same model.

### Entities with different revenue streams

If your entities have different revenue models (one sells software subscriptions, another delivers professional services), build all revenue streams into the consolidated template as separate groups. On each entity sub-sheet, map only the GL accounts relevant to that entity. Rows for revenue streams the entity doesn't have will show zero.

At the consolidated level, all revenue streams appear together and aggregate correctly. On individual entity sub-sheets the zero rows add visual noise, but collapsing the total revenue group removes the bloat. Each entity shows its own revenue cleanly, and the consolidated sheet shows the full picture.

### Single entity with department breakdown

If you operate as a single legal entity, skip the entity breakdown and go straight to a [dimension breakdown](/masterclasses-new/consolidation/dimensions) by department. You get the same per-department forecasting without the consolidation layer.
