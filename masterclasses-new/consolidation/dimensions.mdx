Applying a dimension breakdown gives you planning and reporting below entity level. A sheet breakdown makes your sheet into a template that's deployed across departments, channels, or similar units. A line item breakdown splits a single row by product, project, or any other dimension. See [breakdowns](/features-new/using-francis/breakdowns) for more information.

## Dimensions from your accounting system are automatically available

When you connect your accounting system, Francis detects all dimension values on each journal entry. You can then split sheets and line items by those values, for example by department or project.

When you break down a sheet by dimension, each dimension value gets its own sub-sheet. In the breakdown settings, reached from the three-dot menu next to the breakdown then **Adjust breakdown**, you assign each dimension value to the sub-sheet it feeds. Francis filters the actuals automatically based on that assignment. An **Unallocated** category captures journal entries with no dimension tag.

Assign every dimension value to a sheet for completeness. Some values linger only for redundant reasons and don't need a sheet of their own. Group those into a single **Other** or **Not in use** sub-sheet from **Adjust breakdown**, and the breakdown still includes all actuals correctly.

You can create new sheets for new dimension values at any time. When a new value appears in your accounting system, Francis notifies you to map it to a sheet so the breakdown stays complete. Add sheets from the three-dot menu, **Add sheet**.

On a multi-entity model, apply the entity breakdown first. A dimension breakdown sits under an entity, not the consolidated sheet. Francis treats dimension values locally per accounting system connection: even with a global "department" dimension in a Business Central environment, each entity's department values are considered separately and aren't matched across entities.

## Common use cases

### Department P&L

Break down each entity by department. Each department gets its own sub-sheet for forecasting. Department heads manage their own numbers, the entity rolls them up, and the consolidated sheet rolls up all entities. This is the most common use of dimension breakdowns.

Split the P&L first by entity and then by department. The balance sheet and cash flow are only split by entity, since departmental splits rarely apply below the P&L.

### Departments as cost centres

If departments are used as cost centres, the breakdown only makes sense for OPEX. Revenue, COGS, and below-EBITDA items are not owned at department level. The P&L template still carries those lines, so department sub-sheets will show zero for revenue, COGS, and anything below EBITDA.

Accept the zero rows. They roll up correctly and won't affect totals. The department sub-sheet becomes an OPEX planning view by default, which is usually exactly what department heads need. The entity and consolidated sheets show the full P&L.

### Revenue by product

Apply a line item breakdown to the revenue row using product as the dimension. Each product gets its own sub-row. If you also apply the same breakdown to COGS, the gross profit row at the parent level reflects gross profit per product automatically, without a separate sheet or calculation.

### CAPEX by project

Apply a line item breakdown to the CAPEX row using project as the dimension. Each project gets its own sub-row for the investment amount. This keeps the balance sheet clean while giving you project-level visibility on where capital is being deployed.

### Channel reporting

Break down each entity by channel (retail, wholesale, e-commerce). Use this when revenue mix and margin profile differ significantly by channel and you want channel-level forecasting alongside the entity view.
