Applying a dimension breakdown gives you planning and reporting below entity level. A sheet breakdown deploys your model across departments, channels, or similar units. A line item breakdown splits a single row by product, project, or any other dimension. See [Breakdowns](/features-new/using-francis/breakdowns) to apply either type.

## Each dimension value gets a sub-sheet

Applying a dimension breakdown to an entity sub-sheet deploys the sheet structure across sub-sheets, one per dimension value. Structure flows top-down: rows and calculations you define on the entity sub-sheet sync automatically to all dimension sub-sheets. Data flows bottom-up: actuals, budget, and forecast data roll up from dimension sub-sheets to the entity sub-sheet.

On a multi-entity model, apply the entity breakdown first. A dimension breakdown sits under an entity sub-sheet, not the consolidated sheet.

## Group inactive dimension values

Not every dimension value needs its own planned sub-sheet. When some values exist in your data but don't require active forecasting, group them into a single "Other" or "Not in use" sub-sheet using **Adjust breakdown**. The underlying actuals still roll up correctly, and your active sub-sheets stay clean.

## Common use cases

### Department P&L

Break down each entity sub-sheet by department. Each department gets its own sub-sheet for forecasting. Department heads manage their own numbers; the entity sub-sheet rolls them up; the consolidated sheet rolls up all entities. This is the most common use of dimension breakdowns.

### Departments as cost centres

If departments are used as cost centres, the breakdown only makes sense for OPEX. Revenue, COGS, and below-EBITDA items are not owned at department level. The P&L template still carries those lines, so department sub-sheets will show zero for revenue, COGS, and anything below EBITDA.

Accept the zero rows. They roll up correctly and won't affect totals. The department sub-sheet becomes an OPEX planning view by default, which is usually exactly what department heads need. The entity sub-sheet and consolidated sheet show the full P&L.

### Revenue by product

Apply a line item breakdown to the revenue row using product as the dimension. Each product gets its own sub-row. If you also apply the same breakdown to COGS, the gross profit row at the parent level reflects gross profit per product automatically, without a separate sheet or calculation.

### CAPEX by project

Apply a line item breakdown to the CAPEX row using project as the dimension. Each project gets its own sub-row for the investment amount. This keeps the balance sheet clean while giving you project-level visibility on where capital is being deployed.

### Channel reporting

Break down entity sub-sheets by channel (retail, wholesale, e-commerce). Use this when revenue mix and margin profile differ significantly by channel and you want channel-level forecasting alongside the entity view.
