Breakdowns split your model so you can plan and review data per sub-unit. You can apply a breakdown to an entire sheet or to a single row.

## How breakdowns work

When you apply a breakdown, Francis creates sub-units: a sub-sheet for a sheet breakdown, a sub-row for a line item breakdown. The parent becomes a read-only roll-up, and the data now lives on the sub-units.

<Warning>
  Creating a breakdown removes forecast values, as the sheet becomes a read-only roll-up. Removing a breakdown removes forecasts entered on the sub-sheets.
</Warning>

## Sheet breakdown vs. line item breakdown

A breakdown applies at one of two levels.

**Sheet breakdown** splits the entire sheet. The sheet becomes a template deployed across sub-units. Use this when you want to apply a template across entities, departments, or similar. An example is creating a P&L template per entity or department.

**Line item breakdown** splits a single row. Use this when only one line needs to be split. Examples include splitting a revenue line by products or splitting CAPEX by projects.

## Sheet breakdown

Sheet breakdowns come in two types: entity and [dimension](/masterclasses-new/consolidation/dimensions). Entity comes first and is always the foundation. Dimension breakdowns, by department, channel, or project, sit under entity sub-sheets.

### Breakdowns create a template

When you break down a sheet, it becomes a template. With this template, you build top-down and work bottom-up.

Structure flows top-down: rows, groups, and calculations defined on the consolidated sheet sync to all sub-sheets automatically. You build the model once, at the top level, and every sub-sheet inherits it.

Forecasting flows bottom-up: actuals and forecasts are entered on the sub-sheets and roll up automatically. You never directly edit row values in the consolidated sheet.

These restrictions ensure that the system stays manageable as the model grows.

### Entity breakdown

The entity breakdown is always the starting point. Apply it to your sheet and Francis creates one sub-sheet per entity, together with a consolidation. The consolidated sheet rolls up all entity sub-sheets automatically. This is what makes per-entity forecasting possible.

### Dimension breakdown

Once entity sub-sheets are in place, you can break each one down further by dimension: department, channel, project, or any dimension your accounting system tracks.

## Line item breakdown

A line item breakdown splits a single row rather than the whole sheet. It's designed for splitting a line into its dimensions: revenue into product groups, or CAPEX into projects.
